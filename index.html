How weekly calorie planning works

A weekly calorie budget is your average daily calorie target multiplied by seven. For example, a target of 1,600 calories per day creates a weekly budget of 11,200 calories. You can distribute that total evenly or vary the daily amounts while keeping the weekly total unchanged.

Understand BMR and TDEE

Basal metabolic rate (BMR) estimates the energy your body uses at rest. Total daily energy expenditure (TDEE) estimates daily energy use after accounting for activity. This planner estimates TDEE by multiplying the BMR input by the selected activity factor. These estimates are starting points, not measurements of exact energy needs.

What is calorie cycling?

Calorie cycling means varying calorie intake from day to day while maintaining a chosen average over a longer period. Some people prefer a higher target on more active days and a lower target on quieter days. The weekly total remains the key budget in this calculator.

Frequently asked questions

Does eating more on one day ruin a calorie deficit?

One higher-calorie day does not determine the whole week's outcome. The total intake over time matters. A weekly budget can help put individual days into context.

How do I calculate a weekly calorie budget?

Multiply your average daily calorie target by seven. For example, 1,800 calories per day equals 12,600 calories per week.

What is the difference between BMR and TDEE?

BMR estimates energy use at rest. TDEE estimates total daily energy use, including activity. TDEE is generally higher than BMR.

Is calorie cycling necessary for weight loss?

No. Calorie cycling is an optional planning approach. A consistent daily target can also work; the approach should be practical and sustainable for the individual.

This calculator is for educational planning and does not provide medical advice or a personalized nutrition prescription. Very low calorie targets may be inappropriate; consult a qualified healthcare professional for individualized guidance.
